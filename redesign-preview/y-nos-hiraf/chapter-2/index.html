The Awakening

Y Galwad · The Calling

The darkness has settled in. Now the old voices are stirring. A knock is heard at doors that no longer exist — and the song that follows is not asking to be invited in.

“Some warnings are not spoken. They are sung — and they expect an answer.”

The knock at the threshold

The Mari Lwyd appears at abandoned homes and doorways that have vanished from the map. She does not arrive as a decoration or a seasonal visitor. In this story, she is a presence carrying a warning through the old exchange of pwnco.

Her verses contain fragments of an ancient prophecy. The words are incomplete, but the meaning is unmistakable: the darkness is not simply a night that has lasted too long. Something has called the old powers into motion.

The call goes out

Gwyn ap Nudd summons the forgotten inhabitants of Annwn. Across the boundary, things that have been quiet for generations begin to answer. Whether they come to help, to witness, or to claim something of their own remains uncertain.

The Mari Lwyd's song continues. What is it trying to warn us about — and who is meant to hear the final verse?

Folklore note

The Mari Lwyd

The Mari Lwyd and the pwnco draw on a genuine Welsh winter wassailing tradition. The prophecy and events in this chapter are original Bydysawd fiction.
