When the Welsh settlers of Y Wladfa arrived on the Patagonian plains in 1865, the land had its own creatures. He arrived in Wales just before the First World War with three cousins, a recipe for a coffee that would ruin you for all other coffee, and ambitions the valleys were not quite prepared for. 💀 Weakness: A health inspector requiring him to explain where exactly the restaurant is located. He has never successfully done this.

THE NO MEAT PRINCE OF CAER CROMLECH
TYLWYTH Y TÂN

The last native Prince of Wales — Owain Glyndŵr — disappeared in 1415 without dying. Welsh historians note this with the specific tone of someone who knows exactly what that means. He was never captured. Never confirmed dead. Simply gone, into the mountains, into legend. He became this. Three parts revolutionary prince who raised a Welsh parliament and terrified an English king — one part something that has been doing extreme things to its body since approximately 1999 and has not slowed down. The crown is still there, technically, though it now sits on a snapback cap bearing a leek emblem. The dragon banner he carried into battle has been redrawn in kale.

He has vegan superpowers of devastating and specific force. He can summon a wall of root vegetables that moves at speed. He can weaponise oat milk at range. He can call upon the spirit of the leek — ancient symbol of Wales, worn into battle at Crécy — and it responds with the full force of a thousand years of Welsh national identity, which hits differently than you might expect. The stunts have not stopped. They have become simultaneously more elaborate, more plant-based, and faster — he does all of it on a skateboard. The board is custom — deck printed with the Owain Glyndŵr dragon banner, wheels made from something sustainable he will explain at length if you ask. He grinds castle walls. He ollies standing stones. He has skateboarded the full length of Caerphilly Castle's south wall at speed whilst eating something that should not be eaten. It was vegan. It was filmed. It made a point. Owain Glyndŵr called the first Welsh parliament at Machynlleth in 1404. Mathonwy called a vegan food festival at the same location in 2019 and considers this a direct continuation of the same project. He is not wrong.
